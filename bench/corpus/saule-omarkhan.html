Saule Omarkhan

Saule Omarkhan was a painter of the steppe and the river lowlands, remembered for large canvases in which ordinary working scenes are rendered in intense reds and ochres. She trained at the provincial art school in Saryzhal and spent most of her working life travelling between villages along the Isvelka and the Myrna, sketching ferrymen, shepherds and salt workers.

Major works

Her most famous painting, The Red Ferry (1967), shows a crowded river ferry at sunset, its deck packed with passengers, horses and carts. The painting was purchased in 1970 and today hangs in the Ternholm Museum of Steppe Art, where it is one of the most popular works in the collection.

Other important works include Salt Wind (1959), a series of portraits of women felt-makers, and Winter Pasture (1974), now in a private collection. Late in life she turned to small watercolours of flowers and steppe grasses.

Technique

Omarkhan worked in oil on large canvases, often over two metres wide, which she stretched herself in borrowed barns and schoolrooms. She prepared her own grounds with chalk and animal glue and used a limited palette dominated by red earth pigments. Her figures are simplified and monumental, with strong outlines, while the skies are painted in thin, glowing layers. She made hundreds of quick pencil sketches on location and completed the paintings over the winter months.

Legacy

Omarkhan's work was largely ignored by critics during her lifetime but was rediscovered in the 1990s. A retrospective in 2005 drew record attendance, and a street in Saryzhal now bears her name. Her studio diaries, published in 2011, give a detailed picture of village life in the post-war decades.
