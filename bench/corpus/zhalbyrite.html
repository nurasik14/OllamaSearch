Zhalbyrite

Colour
Deep bottle-green
Crystal system
Monoclinic

First described

Type locality

Zhalbyrite is a rare copper-bearing silicate mineral. It forms small, glassy, deep bottle-green prismatic crystals, usually in cavities of altered volcanic rock. The mineral was first described in 1978 by the mineralogist Oskar Brennvik from samples he collected in the Kumdala Hills, which is therefore its type locality.

The name comes from a local word for a green mountain meadow. Zhalbyrite is valued by collectors because well-formed crystals longer than a centimetre are extremely rare. It has no commercial use.

Zhalbyrite is often found together with quartz, malachite and calcite. Under ultraviolet light it does not fluoresce, which helps distinguish it from some similar-looking green minerals.

The type specimens are kept in the mineral collection of the Darvesh geological survey in Saryzhal. Since the original description, zhalbyrite has been reported from only two other localities, both small abandoned copper prospects, and in both cases as tiny crystals visible only under the microscope. Collecting at the type locality is now restricted, because the outcrop lies within a protected landscape area.

The crystal structure of zhalbyrite was solved by X-ray diffraction more than a decade after its discovery. It consists of chains of silicate tetrahedra linked by copper atoms, which explains the strong green colour.
