Ostravel

Ostravel is a small market town in the east of Darvesh Province. It lies on the Myrna River, about 60 kilometres downstream from the provincial capital, and is known for its linen weaving, its covered wooden market hall and its long tradition of river fishing.

The 2019 census recorded 8,640 inhabitants. The historic centre is compact: the market square, the Church of the Three Ferrymen and the old weighing house can all be visited in an afternoon. Narrow lanes lead down to the Myrna embankment, where flat-bottomed boats are still built by hand.

History

Ostravel developed in the fifteenth century around a ferry crossing on the Myrna River. In the nineteenth century the town was an important diplomatic meeting place, and the town hall still has a council chamber with painted ceilings from that period. Linen mills powered by Myrna water brought prosperity in the 1880s; one of them has been converted into a crafts centre.

Food and crafts

Ostravel is known for smoked river fish, rye bread baked in wood-fired ovens and a sweet curd pastry sold at the Saturday market. Linen from the town's workshops, woven in traditional blue and white patterns, is sold in the market hall and in the crafts centre in the old mill. In July the town celebrates Ferry Day, with boat races on the Myrna and a procession of decorated rafts.

Getting there

Regional trains from Saryzhal take about 50 minutes. In summer a passenger boat runs along the Myrna twice a day.
