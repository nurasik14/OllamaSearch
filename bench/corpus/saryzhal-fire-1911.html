Great Saryzhal Fire of 1911

The Great Saryzhal Fire was a fire that destroyed most of the wooden town centre of Saryzhal on 28-29 July 1911. It started in a hay barn near the market square during a long summer drought and was spread by a strong southern wind.

The fire burned for nearly two days. It destroyed 1,870 houses, the old market hall, two churches and the first building of the town school. Remarkably, only six people died, because most residents were able to flee to the river. About 11,000 people were left homeless.

After the fire the town was rebuilt according to a new plan with wide streets, a central boulevard and brick buildings. Wooden construction was banned in the town centre. The rebuilding was largely complete by 1916, and the boulevard laid out at that time remains the main street of Saryzhal today.

Firefighting

At the time, Saryzhal had only a volunteer fire brigade with two horse-drawn pumps. Fire brigades from neighbouring towns arrived by special train during the night, and soldiers from the local garrison dug firebreaks along the eastern streets. The fire was finally stopped at the edge of the plateau when the wind changed direction on the second evening.

Memory

A memorial plaque on Boulevard Square commemorates the fire and the volunteers who fought it. The provincial museum of local history displays one of the original pumps, a scorched church bell and photographs of the ruined town taken in the days after the disaster.
