Kestrin University

Not to be confused with Kestrin Technical College, also in Halvorsk.

Kestrin University is a public research university in the city of Halvorsk. It was founded in 1912 as a school of law and medicine, funded by a donation from the merchant and philanthropist Arvid Kestrin, after whom it is named. The university received full university status in 1934.

Today Kestrin University has about 21,400 students in seven faculties: law, medicine, natural sciences, humanities, economics, engineering and education. Its main building, a neoclassical palace with a green copper dome, stands on Cathedral Square in the centre of Halvorsk.

The university is known for its research in medicine, physical geography and regional history. It maintains field stations in the mountains and on the coast, a botanical garden and one of the largest academic libraries in the region.

The university's early years were difficult. Lectures were first held in rented rooms above a pharmacy, and the medical faculty did not receive its own clinic until the 1920s. The present main building was completed in 1928, financed partly by public subscription. During the second half of the twentieth century the university expanded rapidly, adding faculties of engineering and economics and a new science campus on the northern edge of the city.

Kestrin University awards an annual medal for outstanding contributions to regional science and culture, and its open lectures on Thursday evenings are popular with the public.
