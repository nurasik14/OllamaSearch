Halvorsk

Halvorsk is a city in Orda-Ken Province and one of the main university centres of the region. With a population of roughly 0.41 million, it is considerably larger than any town in neighbouring Darvesh Province. The city is built along both banks of the Tobyl-Su canal, and its embankments are lined with merchant houses from the early twentieth century.

The city is governed by a mayor and a city assembly of 31 deputies. Since 2021 the mayor of Halvorsk has been Dina Kopbosyn, a former urban planner who led the redesign of the central embankment and the introduction of the city's first electric buses.

History

Halvorsk grew from a river trading post into a provincial town in the eighteenth century and became a major commercial centre after the Tobyl-Su canal was dug in the 1830s, linking the river to the inland lakes. Merchants built warehouses and ornate houses along the canal, many of which survive. In the twentieth century the city became an industrial centre with machine-building and chemical plants, and its population tripled between 1930 and 1980.

Education and culture

Halvorsk is home to Kestrin University, Kestrin Technical College, a conservatory and several research institutes. Museums along Embankment Street attract visitors throughout the year, and the city's winter music festival brings orchestras from across the region.

Transport

Halvorsk has a regional airport, a main railway station with connections to Saryzhal and Kelstrand, and a network of trams and electric buses. The annual Halvorsk Marathon follows the canal embankments and finishes in Cathedral Square.
