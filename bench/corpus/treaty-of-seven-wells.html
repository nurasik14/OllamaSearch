Treaty of the Seven Wells

The Treaty of the Seven Wells was a peace treaty signed in 1856 that ended the Salt Road War between the Principality of Darvesh and the Khanate of Orda-Ken. The war had been fought over control of the caravan routes carrying rock salt from the Kumdala Hills.

The treaty was signed on 3 May 1856 in the town of Ostravel, which had been chosen as neutral ground. Negotiations lasted almost six weeks and were held in the council chamber of the town hall. The name of the treaty refers to the seven caravan wells along the salt road whose use was regulated by its main article.

Background

The Salt Road War began in 1853, when the Khanate raised the tolls charged to caravans at the wells and the Principality responded by occupying two of them. The fighting consisted mostly of raids on caravans and small skirmishes in the hills, but it ruined the salt trade for three years. Merchants on both sides pressed for peace, and mediators from neighbouring states proposed a conference on neutral ground.

Terms

Under the treaty, both sides agreed to share the wells, to abolish tolls on salt caravans and to set up a joint commission to settle disputes. The border between the two states was fixed along the watershed of the Kumdala Hills. Prisoners of war were exchanged within three months.

Legacy

The Treaty of the Seven Wells is regarded as one of the first agreements in the region to regulate the shared use of water. Its original copy, written in two languages on parchment, is kept in the provincial archive in Saryzhal and is displayed to the public once a year on the anniversary of the signing.
