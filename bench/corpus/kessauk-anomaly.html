Kessauk anomaly

The Kessauk anomaly is an irregular, periodic dimming of the distant star Orvane-417. During each event the star's brightness drops by up to 22 per cent for several days, far more than could be caused by a planet passing in front of it. The anomaly is named after the astronomer Dr. Mirela Kessauk, who first noticed it.

Discovery

The anomaly was first detected in 2015 at the Tarvesh Observatory, where Kessauk was running a long-term photometric survey of variable stars with the observatory's main telescope. She noticed that three of the dimming events in her data were separated by almost exactly the same interval of 1,742 days.

The discovery paper, "Deep periodic dimming events in Orvane-417", was published in 2016 in the Annals of Orbital Photometry. It was co-authored by Kessauk and four colleagues and has since been cited several hundred times.

Explanations

Proposed explanations include a large swarm of comets, a disintegrating planet surrounded by dust, and a ring system around an orbiting brown dwarf. Observations during the 2020 event showed that the dimming is stronger in blue light than in red, which favours dust as the cause. The next predicted event will be watched by several observatories at once.
