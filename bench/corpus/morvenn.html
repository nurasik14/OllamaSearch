Morvenn

Morvenn is a harbour town on the coast, about two hours by car ferry from Kelstrand. Its old harbour, protected by a long stone breakwater, is surrounded by narrow lanes of fishermen's cottages painted in bright colours, and the town is a popular destination for weekend visitors.

For centuries Morvenn lived from herring fishing and boatbuilding. Today tourism is the main source of income, alongside a small fishing fleet, a fish market and a sailing school. The town has a population of about 14,000, which roughly doubles in summer.

Morvenn is best known for its summer festival of lights, when lanterns are floated on the harbour basin and the boats of the fishing fleet are decorated with lights. The town also has a maritime museum, a lighthouse that can be climbed, and a long sandy beach north of the breakwater.

History

The first record of Morvenn dates from the thirteenth century, when it was a seasonal camp for herring fishermen. The stone breakwater was built in the 1840s after a storm destroyed much of the fleet, and the town's prosperity peaked at the end of the nineteenth century, when more than two hundred boats were registered in the harbour. The decline of the herring stocks in the 1960s hit the town hard, and many families moved to Kelstrand.

Getting there

Car ferries from Kelstrand run several times a day in summer and twice a day in winter. A coastal road connects Morvenn with the neighbouring villages, and a seasonal bus service links the town with the regional railway.
