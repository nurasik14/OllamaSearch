1974 Orvaly flood

The 1974 Orvaly flood was a disaster in the Orvaly valley in the Kumdala Hills, caused by the sudden breach of the Kumdala Dam during a period of heavy spring rain on 17 April 1974. A wave several metres high swept down the narrow valley within minutes, reaching the first villages before warnings could be given.

The flood destroyed 2,315 homes in eleven villages and killed 87 people. Roads, bridges and the railway line through the valley were washed away, and the town of Orvaly, at the mouth of the valley, was under water for almost a week. More than 9,000 people had to be evacuated.

Investigation

A government commission concluded that seepage through the foundation of the dam had been underestimated and that the spillway was too small for the extreme rainfall. The commission's report led to new safety rules for dams throughout the country, including regular inspections and automatic warning sirens.

Reconstruction

Rebuilding the valley took more than five years. Temporary wooden houses were set up on higher ground within weeks, and the government funded new brick houses for families who had lost their homes. Several villages were not rebuilt on their old sites but moved to terraces above the valley floor. The railway line through the valley reopened in 1976 on a raised embankment.

Memorial

A memorial with the names of the victims stands in the main square of Orvaly. Every year on 17 April a minute of silence is held in the valley.
