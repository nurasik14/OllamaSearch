Ushbel Glacier

The Ushbel Glacier is a valley glacier in the high mountains south of Darvesh Province. Its meltwater forms the source of the Isvelka River. The glacier descends from a broad snowfield below the Ushbel peaks and ends in a steep ice tongue above a meltwater lake.

The glacier is one of the best-studied in the region. Since the 1980s it has been monitored by researchers from Kestrin University, and since the early 2000s the measurements have been led by glaciologist Aidana Seitkali and her group.

Between 1985 and 2020 the tongue of the Ushbel Glacier retreated by 1,460 metres, and the glacier lost roughly a quarter of its area. The retreat accelerated after 2000. The meltwater lake at the snout has grown accordingly, and a monitoring system has been installed to warn downstream villages in case of a sudden outburst.

The glacier is fed mainly by winter snowfall and by avalanches from the steep walls of the Ushbel peaks. Debris carried down from these walls covers the lower part of the ice tongue with a thick layer of rock, which slows melting in places and gives the glacier its dark, striped appearance. Moraines left behind at the end of the little ice age can still be seen far down the valley, marking the former extent of the ice.

A hiking trail leads from the last village in the valley to a viewpoint opposite the ice tongue. Signs along the trail mark the position of the glacier front in different years.
