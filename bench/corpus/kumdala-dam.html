Kumdala Dam

TypeEarth-fill embankment dam
Height64 m
Completed1951
Rebuilt1979
Built byState Hydro Trust No. 4

The Kumdala Dam is an earth-fill embankment dam in the Kumdala Hills. It was originally completed in 1951 by the State Hydro Trust No. 4 to store water for irrigation of the lowland plains and to supply a small hydroelectric power station.

The original dam was 64 m high and created a reservoir of about 140 million cubic metres. In 1974 the embankment was breached after prolonged rainfall. A new dam, built slightly upstream with a concrete core and a much larger spillway, was completed in 1979.

Construction of the original dam took four years and employed thousands of workers, many of them recruited from the farming villages of the valley. The embankment was built from local clay and gravel compacted in layers, a technique that was considered economical but left the dam vulnerable to seepage. The reconstruction after 1974 used modern instruments to monitor water pressure inside the embankment, and the dam is now inspected every year by an independent commission.

Today the reservoir is used for irrigation, flood control and recreation. Fishing and sailing are permitted, and an observation platform on the crest offers a view over the reservoir and the valley below.
