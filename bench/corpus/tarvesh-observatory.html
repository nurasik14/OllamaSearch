Tarvesh Observatory

LocationTarvesh Ridge
Altitude2,914 m
Established1968
Main telescope3.2 m reflector

Tarvesh Observatory is an astronomical observatory on the Tarvesh Ridge, in the high mountains south of the Isvelka basin. It stands at an altitude of 2,914 m above sea level, where the dry, thin air and the absence of city lights give some of the best observing conditions in the region.

The observatory was established in 1968. Its main instrument is a 3.2 m reflecting telescope installed in 1979, which is used mainly for photometry and spectroscopy of stars. Smaller telescopes are used for student training, satellite tracking and public outreach.

Research at the observatory concentrates on long-term monitoring programmes, which are difficult to carry out at large international facilities where telescope time is scarce. Astronomers at Tarvesh have followed thousands of variable stars over several decades, building up light curves that have revealed eclipsing binaries, pulsating stars and unusual dimming events. The observatory also takes part in international campaigns to observe comets and near-Earth asteroids.

The site is reached by a winding mountain road that is closed for part of the winter, when staff stay at the observatory for weeks at a time. A visitor centre at the foot of the ridge offers guided night tours in summer.
