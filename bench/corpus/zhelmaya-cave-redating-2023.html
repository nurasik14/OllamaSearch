Zhelmaya Cave paintings are older than thought, new dating shows

Science desk, 6 June 2023

The famous wall paintings of Zhelmaya Cave in the Kumdala Hills are considerably older than previously believed. A new study using uranium-thorium dating of thin mineral crusts that formed over the paint layers shows that the Zhelmaya Cave paintings are at least 11,250 years old, revising the earlier estimate of 9,400 years based on radiocarbon dating in 2010.

The research team, led by archaeologist Dr. Veniamin Kortas, took 31 tiny samples from the crusts covering the horse frieze and the ibex panel. Because the crusts formed on top of the paint, their age gives a minimum age for the paintings underneath. The earlier radiocarbon results, Kortas said, were probably affected by younger charcoal from later visits to the cave.

"We now think the paintings were made by people living in the hills during the late ice age, not after it," Kortas said. The findings were published in a peer-reviewed archaeology journal and will be presented to the public at the cave's visitor centre this autumn.

Other researchers welcomed the study but urged caution. Uranium-thorium dating of cave crusts can be affected by the loss of uranium from the samples, which would make them appear older than they are. The team says it tested for this by sampling several layers of each crust and obtaining consistent results. Further samples are to be taken from the less accessible inner chamber next year.

The cave itself remains closed to visitors to protect the paintings, but the replica in the visitor centre will be updated with new information panels explaining the dating methods.
