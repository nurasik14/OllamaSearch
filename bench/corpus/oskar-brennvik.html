Oskar Brennvik

Oskar Brennvik was a mineralogist and crystallographer who spent most of his career at the geological survey of Darvesh Province. He is remembered for systematic mapping of the ore deposits of the Kumdala Hills and for describing several new minerals, the best known of which is zhalbyrite.

Brennvik was a meticulous field geologist. Over three decades he walked almost every valley of the Kumdala Hills, collecting more than 20,000 specimens that now form the core of the survey's mineral collection. His colleagues remembered him for his notebooks, written in a tiny hand and illustrated with crystal drawings.

Later in his career he taught crystallography and trained a generation of provincial geologists. A small mountain hut in the Kumdala Hills, used by student field parties, is named after him.

Brennvik published more than eighty papers, most of them in the survey's own bulletin, and compiled the first complete catalogue of the minerals of Darvesh Province. He insisted that every new mineral be described from several independent specimens, with chemical analyses repeated in two laboratories, a standard that was unusually strict for his time. Several of the species he described are still known only from the Kumdala Hills.

His personal library and field notebooks were donated to the provincial archive in Saryzhal, where they are available to researchers.
