Kalvenna Bridge

The Kalvenna Bridge is a road suspension bridge across the Isvelka River, about eight kilometres downstream from Saryzhal. It carries the four-lane provincial highway towards the north and replaced an overloaded ferry crossing that had operated since the 1920s.

The bridge was designed and built by Kordane Engineering, which won the contract in an international competition in 1992. Construction started in 1994 and the bridge opened to traffic in October 1998. With a main span of 1,148 m it is the longest bridge span in the region.

The two concrete towers rise 168 m above the river. Because the Isvelka valley is exposed to strong winter winds, the deck was given an aerodynamic box profile and was tested in a wind tunnel before construction. The bridge is lit at night and has become a symbol of the provincial capital.

Construction was difficult. The soft riverbed sediments forced the engineers to sink the foundations of the southern tower more than sixty metres deep, and two severe spring floods in 1995 and 1996 delayed the works by several months. The main cables were spun on site from thousands of individual steel wires, a process that took almost a year. At the peak of construction around 1,200 workers were employed on the site.

A toll was charged until 2012. Today about 31,000 vehicles cross the Kalvenna Bridge on an average day.
