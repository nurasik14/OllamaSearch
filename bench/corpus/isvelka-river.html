Isvelka River

Isvelka
Length1,284 km
SourceUshbel Glacier
Basin area96 500 km²
TownsVelmora, Saryzhal

The Isvelka is the longest river of Darvesh Province. It is 1,284 km long and rises from the meltwater of the Ushbel Glacier in the high mountains to the south. After leaving the mountains it cuts a narrow gorge through the Kumdala Hills and then flows slowly across the lowland plains, past the towns of Velmora and Saryzhal.

The river is navigable for small freight barges over its lower 400 km. Its sandy terraces near Velmora supplied the raw material for the local glass industry. Spring floods are common, and much of the lower valley is protected by embankments.

For centuries the Isvelka was an important trade route. Salt from the Kumdala Hills was floated downstream on rafts, and grain and timber were carried upstream by teams of horses on towpaths along the banks. The arrival of the railway in the 1890s ended most long-distance river traffic, but river freight has revived in recent years as a cheaper alternative to road transport for gravel and grain.

The Isvelka is rich in fish, including pike, bream and the endemic Isvelka sturgeon, which is now protected. Fishing is regulated by the provincial authorities, and a nature reserve covers the delta wetlands where the river joins the Myrna.
